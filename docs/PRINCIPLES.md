# Principles: what makes a small story game good

Every role reads this. It says what good looks like, so a fresh thread can judge its own work. The owner's accepted
rules are marked **(owner)** with a date. Change it by pull request, like the lessons files.

## The story

1. **Every scene gives the player something to do or to dread.** A scene that only delivers information is cut
   or folded into one that does.
2. **Show, don't lecture.** No scene exists to deliver a message. Meaning comes from what people do.
3. **People are defined by choices and history.** Each character on paper has a want, a secret or contradiction,
   a voice, and an ending.
4. **The ending pays off the opening.** Write the last scene early; every scene earns it.
5. **Choices change something the player can see.** If two options lead to the same place, say so or merge them.
6. **Original.** Names, places and plots are our own. No real brands, no recreations of existing games.

## The look

7. **Outline art reads by silhouette.** Big shapes first (blocks, towers, the road), then rhythm (floors,
   windows, balconies), then a few hand-placed details. Detail without rhythm reads as noise.
8. **Line budget, not polygon budget.** Cost is the number of lines on screen. Merge each area into one lines mesh
   and one faces mesh; fade small details with distance before the view gets busy.
9. **Judge it at eye height.** Pictures for review are taken from where the player stands, not from above.

## The build

10. **Small enough to finish.** A hard length target per format; scope cuts happen in the plan, not at the end.
11. **Build in stages and prove each one.** Each stage has a proof table: automated tests plus the owner's own
    try. A vertical slice (one scene, start to finish, real art) comes before the rest.
12. **Data, not code.** Every number, colour and line of text lives in `data/`, so tuning and writing never need
    a code change.
13. **Picture first.** The owner sees a picture before anything big is built and judges from pictures after.
